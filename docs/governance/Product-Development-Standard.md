# JERTINA Product Development Standard

## Version 1.0

### Purpose

This standard defines the minimum requirements for every product developed, sourced, improved, or sold under the JERTINA brand.

No product should enter the portfolio without meeting these standards.

---

# Product Lifecycle

Every product follows this sequence:

1. Opportunity Identification
2. Customer Research
3. Problem Definition
4. Market Validation
5. Product Requirements
6. Supplier Evaluation
7. Sample Procurement
8. Testing & Evaluation
9. Improvement
10. Launch Approval
11. Production
12. Post-Launch Review
13. Continuous Improvement
14. Retirement (when appropriate)

No stage may be skipped without documented approval.

---

# Entry Criteria

Before a product enters development it must have:

* A clearly defined customer problem.
* Evidence of market demand.
* Alignment with the JERTINA brand.
* A documented business case.
* Preliminary supplier research.

---

# Quality Requirements

Every product must meet documented standards for:

* Functionality
* Durability
* Safety
* Ease of Use
* Packaging Quality
* Manufacturing Consistency
* Customer Satisfaction

Quality requirements should be measurable whenever possible.

---

# Product Evaluation

Each product should be evaluated against criteria such as:

* Brand fit
* Customer value
* Competitive differentiation
* Margin potential
* Operational feasibility
* Scalability
* Risk profile

Products that do not meet the minimum acceptance threshold should be revised or rejected.

---

# Supplier Standards

Suppliers should be assessed using documented criteria, including:

* Product quality
* Manufacturing capability
* Communication
* Lead times
* Pricing
* Reliability
* Compliance with applicable laws and regulations

Supplier performance should be reviewed regularly.

---

# Testing Standard

Before launch:

* Product samples should be inspected.
* Functional testing should be completed.
* Packaging should be evaluated.
* Documentation should be verified.
* Customer-facing materials should accurately reflect the product.

---

# Launch Approval

A product may proceed to launch only after review by the relevant executive roles defined in the Executive Governance Charter.

Any unresolved critical issues should delay launch until addressed.

---

# Continuous Improvement

After launch, customer feedback, returns, warranty claims, support requests, and performance metrics should be reviewed on a recurring basis.

Documented improvements should be prioritized before expanding the product line.

---

# Retirement

Products may be retired when they:

* No longer meet quality expectations,
* No longer align with the brand,
* Become commercially unsustainable, or
* Are replaced by a superior solution.

Retirement decisions should be documented to preserve organizational knowledge.
